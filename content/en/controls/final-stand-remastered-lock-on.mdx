Missed attacks in a crowded fight usually aren't a skill problem — they're a targeting problem. In Final Stand Remastered, the lock on system decides whether your combo lands on the boss or on the grunt standing in front of it, and that single decision can mean the difference between a clean clear and a death screen. Getting Final Stand Remastered lock on under control makes every dodge, parry, and heavy attack easier to time.

This guide breaks down how targeting behaves, which inputs control it, which settings are worth changing, and how to fix the lock on problems players report most often. Whether you're on keyboard and mouse or a controller, you'll finish with a setup that keeps your camera pointed where your damage actually needs to go.

## What Lock On Actually Does in Final Stand Remastered

Lock on is more than a camera toggle. It's a package of small assists that work together: soft camera tracking, attack redirection, and target selection. When it's on, your character turns toward the chosen enemy and your directional inputs get interpreted relative to that enemy rather than to the world.

That matters most in two situations. First, when multiple enemies overlap, because a free camera will happily send your heavy attack into the wrong body. Second, when a boss moves fast, because manual camera correction costs you the reaction time you need for a dodge.

| Feature | What it changes | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Target snap | Points camera and attacks at one enemy | Stops whiffs when you're surrounded |
| Soft camera follow | Tracks enemy movement smoothly | Keeps the target on screen during dashes |
| Attack correction | Nudges your input toward the target | Combos land while you strafe |
| Target cycling | Moves the reticle between enemies | Lets you switch to a priority threat fast |
| Release / zoom out | Frees the camera | Needed for repositioning and group dodges |

A useful way to think about it: lock on trades camera freedom for attack accuracy. Knowing when to accept that trade is the real skill.

## Default Lock On Controls by Input Device

Exact bindings vary by platform, patch, and whether you've remapped anything, so treat the table below as the common layout rather than a guarantee. Always confirm your own binds in the game's Options or Settings menu under Controls before you commit muscle memory to them.

| Action | Keyboard and mouse | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Engage lock on | Middle mouse button (or a remapped face key) | Right stick click (R3) |
| Release lock on | Same button again, or a dedicated cancel key | Right stick click again |
| Cycle targets | Mouse wheel or adjacent keys | Right stick left / right |
| Free camera | Mouse movement | Right stick movement |
| Recenter camera | Camera reset key | Camera reset button |
| Zoom or pull back | Scroll wheel | Trigger or stick modifier |

Two patterns show up across nearly every version of the game. First, the same button both engages and releases lock on — that's a toggle, not a hold. Second, cycling is bound to the same input you use for camera movement, which is why the camera sometimes feels like it's fighting you mid-fight.

If your controller has a notoriously clicky stick button, remapping lock on to a shoulder button is one of the highest-value changes you can make. Community reports consistently mention stick-click fatigue during long boss attempts.

## Settings That Change How Your Lock On Feels

The controls menu is only half the story. The options menu is where lock on behavior actually gets tuned, and a few sliders do most of the work.

| Setting | Recommended starting point | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lock on mode | Toggle | Saves thumb and finger strain over long sessions |
| Target priority | Camera center, then nearest | Matches what your eyes are already tracking |
| Camera follow strength | Medium | High values fight manual corrections |
| Camera speed | 60–75% of max | Keeps tracking readable during fast boss moves |
| Aim assist / magnetism | On if available | Helps correction without removing input |
| Stick dead zone | As low as comfortable | Reduces the "nothing happens" zone on small nudges |

The single most misunderstood setting is camera follow strength. Turning it all the way up sounds like it would make targeting easier, but it usually makes it worse: the camera keeps recentering while you're trying to nudge it, and the resulting tug-of-war feels like input lag. Medium is the sweet spot for most players.

For a game with this much combat density, it's also worth double-checking performance and platform details before you start tuning. Patch notes and platform information are usually posted on the [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/) for the game, and a locked frame rate makes camera tracking noticeably more consistent.

## Step-by-Step: Locking On, Switching, and Breaking Off

Treat lock on as a three-part loop rather than a single button. The players who clear hard content cleanly are the ones who consciously move between these phases.

| Step | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scan the arena before engaging | You know how many targets exist |
| 2 | Engage lock on the primary threat | Attacks and camera commit to one enemy |
| 3 | Confirm the reticle sits on the right body | Prevents wasted openings |
| 4 | Cycle targets when a new threat closes in | Keeps pressure on the correct enemy |
| 5 | Release lock on before wide dodges | Restores full camera control |
| 6 | Re-engage after the dodge | Returns accuracy without losing position |

A practical habit: release lock on any time you need to move more than a short dash away from your target. Holding it through a big reposition is the most common cause of "why did my camera do that" complaints.

Another habit worth building is confirming your target visually before committing a slow heavy attack. The snap assist will point you at *something* — it's on you to make sure that something is the enemy you actually want to hit.

## Common Lock On Problems and How to Fix Them

Here's a troubleshooting table built from the issues players ask about most. Fixes are ordered from quickest to most involved.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lock on won't engage | Target out of range, invulnerable, or mid-animation | Wait for the opening, then re-engage |
| Reticle flips between two enemies | Two targets at similar camera distance | Switch target priority to camera center; approach from an angle |
| Camera feels sluggish or laggy | Follow strength too high, or unstable frame rate | Lower follow strength; cap frame rate |
| Attacks still miss | Input relative to camera, not target | Confirm correction is enabled; reduce strafe input on heavies |
| Controller bind doesn't respond | Stick click worn or remapped | Move lock on to a shoulder button |
| Lock drops mid-combo | Attacking out of range or taking a hit | Shorten the combo or close distance first |

If a fix doesn't stick, reset your control scheme to default and rebuild it one change at a time. Layered remaps are a hidden source of targeting weirdness, and a clean baseline makes the culprit obvious.

## Advanced Tips for Harder Encounters

Once the basics are automatic, these habits separate comfortable clears from messy ones.

- **Use lock on as a threat tracker, not a leash.** It tells you where your chosen enemy is; it shouldn't stop you from watching the rest of the arena.
- **Cycle before you attack, not after.** Retarget during recovery frames so your next combo starts on the right body.
- **Break lock to bait.** Releasing lock on can pull the camera wide and reveal off-screen enemies that were about to punish you.
- **Practice release timing on trash mobs** rather than bosses, where a mistake is cheap.
- **Keep sensitivity consistent across saves.** Rebuilding muscle memory after a settings change costs more than most players expect.

Player experience also suggests that group encounters get much easier once you stop treating lock on as permanently on. The strongest approach reported by the community is a rhythm: lock, burst, release, reposition, re-lock. That rhythm keeps your damage targeted without trapping your camera.

## FAQ

### Does Final Stand Remastered lock on work the same on PC and console?

Functionally yes — the system does the same job on every platform. What changes is the binding, since controller lock on typically lives on the right stick click while PC players usually get a mouse button or a key. Confirm your exact layout in the controls menu, since defaults can shift between patches.

### Can I turn aim assist or lock on off completely?

Most builds let you adjust or disable the assisting parts of targeting, though options vary. If you prefer full manual camera control, lower follow strength first and disable assist second — an all-or-nothing switch tends to feel worse than a gradual adjustment while you adapt.

### Why does my target keep switching on its own?

Automatic target switching usually happens when two enemies are at nearly the same camera distance, so the priority logic keeps re-evaluating. Set target priority to camera center, approach the group from an angle so targets separate, and manually cycle instead of letting the system choose.

### Is Final Stand Remastered lock on better with a controller?

It depends on what you're fighting. Controllers tend to feel smoother for sustained tracking and dodges because the stick gives continuous camera input, while keyboard and mouse give faster target cycling and more precise camera snaps in crowded arenas. If you have the option, try both against the same boss.

## Wrapping Up

The short version: engage lock on for accuracy, release it for mobility, and tune follow strength and target priority before blaming your reflexes. Most targeting frustration in Final Stand Remastered comes from settings and habits, not from the system itself.

Start by resetting your controls to default, set target priority to camera center, drop follow strength to medium, and practice the lock-burst-release rhythm on regular enemies. Do that for a few sessions and your Final Stand Remastered lock on will stop being something you fight and start being something you use.
