## Understanding the Level Cap and Transfer Cap

Two numbers do most of the work in shaping how you play: 305 and 125. The level cap of 305 is the ceiling the developers currently advertise for character progression. Read it as a boundary, not a roadmap. It tells you how far progression can go, not how fast you will get there or which activities are most efficient.

The transfer cap of 125 is the figure the official page publishes for transfers. Community players commonly describe it as the level threshold that matters when moving progress between servers or carrying a character forward, but the official description does not spell out the mechanics. If the exact behavior of transfers affects your plans, confirm it in-game before you build a strategy around it. The safest reading is the literal one: 125 is the transfer cap the developers advertise, and any deeper explanation is community interpretation.

For a remaster, these numbers also signal intent. Rather than resetting everything from the original Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the team has kept an expansion framework and a defined cap structure. That is consistent with a remaster that preserves the classic loop while rebuilding it.

## What Community Guides Add, and How to Weigh Them

Third-party guides are useful for orientation, not authority. 1. Read the official [Final Stand Remastered page](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) first, so you know the confirmed facts.

3. Verify in-game by checking menus, tooltips, and your own character sheet. If a number is not visible, it is probably not documented.
4. Check the developer group's announcements for updates, since the place was last updated on October 12, 2026, and live games change.

This is especially important with codes. The official page documents no promo-code system. That does not mean codes can never exist, but it means no code list should be presented as confirmed until the developers publish one.
