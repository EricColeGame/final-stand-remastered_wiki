## The level cap, the transfer cap, and why they matter together

Two numbers dominate how you plan a character: a level cap of 305 and a transfer cap of 125. The level cap is straightforward enough as a ceiling, but the transfer cap is the more interesting figure, because it implies a system where progress can move between characters or slots up to a limit. The official page does not explain the mechanic, and no developer documentation describes how transfers are performed, what they cost, or what restrictions apply.

That silence matters. Community guides discuss transfer behavior, but they are secondary observations written by players, not developer documentation, and they go stale quickly after updates. The game was last updated on October 12, 2026, two days before the record used here, so any guide older than that may describe a version of the game that no longer exists.

If you want to know how transfers actually work in your session, the reliable method is to open the game itself and read the in-game interface or ask in a server. Do not assume a number you read in a guide matches the live build.

## The Namek expansion and the DBZ source material

Namek is the current expansion, which places the game's content arc somewhere in the Frieza saga territory of the source material the developers name. Dragon Ball Z is the inspiration, and the credited wiki documents the races, forms, and terminology the game draws on. What the official page does not do is specify which races are playable, what transformations exist, how they unlock, or what any of them do numerically.

That distinction is important, because anime RPG communities generate enormous amounts of speculative content. You will find pages claiming specific stat multipliers, transformation unlock levels, and quest chains. None of that is developer documentation. Treat it as rumor until you confirm it in-game.

The practical approach for a new player is to pick a race based on how it feels to play for an hour, not based on a spreadsheet someone posted. Since no official source publishes race statistics, there is no authoritative basis for ranking them.

## Leveling from 1 to 305: what guides can and cannot tell you

Community guides generally agree on the broad shape of progression: grind enemies, complete quests, and repeat. They disagree on the specifics, which is expected when the underlying numbers are undocumented.

What you can reasonably do:

- Follow the in-game quest markers rather than a written route, since routes change with patches.
- Join servers with active players, since the game supports up to 50, and group content tends to be faster than solo grinding.
- Check the official page update date before following any guide. A guide written before the most recent update may be describing different content.

What you should not do is trust any published experience-per-hour figure, drop rate, or "best grinding spot" list. Those numbers are not in the official description, and the game's developers have not published them anywhere the official record indicates.

## Race, form, and build advice: a caveat

Search for Final Stand Remastered builds and you will find confident recommendations. Almost all of them are third-party guide content built on player testing, not on developer-published values. That does not make them useless, but it does mean they can be wrong, and that they can become wrong overnight.

A reasonable rule: use community builds as a starting suggestion, then verify against your own character's actual numbers in-game. If a guide claims a specific multiplier or unlock level, check whether it cites the official page or a developer channel. If it cites neither, treat it as one player's experience.
