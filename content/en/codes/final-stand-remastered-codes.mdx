Final Stand: Remastered is a Roblox RPG with a longer history than its own listing suggests. The game retells and rebuilds material from Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, an older Roblox RPG that built a following around anime-inspired races, leveling and progression. The remaster carries that structure forward on a newer place file, with a small development team, a stated expansion roadmap, and a level cap that signals how far the current content runs.

Because the game is popular and its systems are number-heavy, a lot of information circulates about it that is not actually confirmed by the developers. This article separates the official record from community observation. ## The remaster question: old game versus new place

The single most useful thing to understand about Final Stand: Remastered is that it is not the original Dragon Ball Z Final Stand. It is a separate experience that rebuilds that game's premise. That distinction matters for several reasons.

Old guides, videos and forum posts about Dragon Ball Z Final Stand may describe systems that the remaster handles differently, or may be pitched at a version of the game that no longer matches. When you search for information, check whether the source is talking about the remaster or the original. The official page only claims remaster status; it does not list a changelog of what was altered, so any detailed comparison you find is somebody else's analysis rather than developer documentation.

It also means the remaster has its own update cadence. The last update shown in the official record is October 12, 2026, two days before this article. That is a recent touch, which is a reasonable sign the project is still being worked on, though the page does not say what changed.

## Races, forms and anime terminology

The developers name Dragon Ball Z as the source material, and the game's races, transformations and terminology follow from that. The official Roblox description does not enumerate races, list transformation stages, or give any stat values. If you see a guide claiming a specific multiplier for a specific form, that number came from testing, datamining or guesswork, not from the developers' own documentation.

Practically, this means you should treat race and form advice as community knowledge. Useful, often accurate, and occasionally outdated after a balance patch. The safe approach is to pick a race based on how you actually want to play, then verify its current behavior in-game rather than trusting a spreadsheet.
