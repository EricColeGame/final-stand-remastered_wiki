## Why the Level Cap and Transfer Cap Are Worth Understanding

A cap of 305 tells you the game has a defined progression ceiling rather than an open-ended grind. A transfer cap of 125 tells you that whatever transfer system the game uses is bounded, not unlimited. The official page does not explain how the transfer cap is applied, whether it resets, or what it gates, so the honest answer is that you should verify it in-game through the interface itself.

Practically, these two numbers are the load-bearing facts for planning. If you are deciding whether to start fresh or continue, the caps tell you how much headroom exists. They do not tell you how long progression takes, which is a detail the developers have not published and community guides vary on.

## What Third-Party Guides Add — and Where They Should Be Treated as Secondary

Community wikis and guide videos are where most of the moment-to-moment advice lives: which races people favor, how forms are used, and how quests flow. These are secondary observations. They are useful for orientation, but they are not developer documentation, and they can lag behind updates.

The credited wiki documents races, forms, and Dragon Ball Z terminology, and the official page names Dragon Ball Z as the source material. That means terms like races and transformations are legitimate to discuss. What is not legitimate is inventing stat values, multipliers, quest steps, or unlock levels. If a guide gives you a multiplier, ask whether the game itself shows it, because the official page does not publish any.

One practical rule: treat any specific number you cannot see in-game as unverified. Level caps and transfer caps are published; drop rates and damage formulas are not.
