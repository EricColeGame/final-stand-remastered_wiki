## Reading the caps correctly

The level cap of 305 and the transfer cap of 125 are the two numbers players ask about most, and they do different jobs. The level cap is the ceiling on character progression for the current expansion. The transfer cap is a separate limit, and the official page does not explain its exact function beyond stating the figure.

That gap is worth respecting. Community discussions often treat the transfer cap as a progression milestone or a gating mechanic, but the official description gives the number without a definition. If you want to know precisely how it applies to your character, check it in the game itself — your character or account panel is the place where the game would surface a limit it enforces. Do not take a third-party explanation as developer documentation, because it is not.

Practical reasoning: treat 305 as the long-term target and 125 as a checkpoint you will encounter well before it. Plan your play around the expansion you are actually in (Namek), not around a future expansion the page does not advertise.

## How to verify anything the page leaves out

The most useful habit for this game is a verification loop, because the official listing is thin and third-party coverage fills the gap with varying accuracy.

1. Open the official Roblox page and read the current description. Expansion, level cap, and transfer cap live there.
2. Check the last-updated date on the page. If it predates a change you heard about, the change may be real but undocumented, or it may be rumor.
3. Test the claim in-game on a live server. Caps, menus, and character options are things the game itself will show you.
4. Check developer group announcements, if the group posts them, before trusting a guide.

This loop costs a few minutes and saves hours of grinding toward a target that does not exist.

## What community guides are good for — and where they mislead

Third-party guides are genuinely helpful for orientation: which activities feel rewarding early, how servers tend to behave, what the general grind looks like at a high level. They are also written by players, often at a specific point in time, and Roblox experiences change without notice. A guide written before the October 12, 2026 update may describe a build that no longer matches.

Where guides mislead most is precision. Specific multipliers, exact drop rates, item prices, and quest step-by-step sequences are the kind of detail that reads as authoritative and ages badly. If a guide states a number the official page does not, treat it as that guide's observation on that day, not as a fact about the current build. Never assume a code list is current when the official page documents no code system at all — if codes exist, a developer channel would be the place to confirm it.
