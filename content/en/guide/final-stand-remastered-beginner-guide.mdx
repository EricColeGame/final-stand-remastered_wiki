## What the Official Page Confirms

The developer-written description is short and specific. It states three things worth repeating exactly:

- The game is a remaster of the classic Roblox RPG *Dragon Ball Z Final Stand*.
- The current expansion is **Namek**.
- The **level cap is 305** and the **transfer cap is 125**.

It also credits the development team by role: lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists and animator.

That is the entire documented feature set. There is no promo-code section, no item shop listing, no skill tree breakdown and no drop-rate table on the official page. If a third-party site presents those things, treat them as secondary observations rather than developer documentation. They may be accurate, out of date, or simply copied from the older game.

The "remaster" framing matters. Because the source material is *Dragon Ball Z Final Stand*, the two games share a lineage, but they are separate places with separate pages. Progress, caps and content do not automatically carry from one to the other, and the official page does not claim any transfer feature between them.

## The Two Numbers New Players Ask About Most

Level cap and transfer cap confuse people because they sound similar but do different jobs.

The **level cap** is the advertised ceiling for character progression in the current build. The official page says 305. That tells you the intended end point of the Namek-era grind, not how fast you reach it.

The **transfer cap** is stated as 125. The official page does not define the mechanic in detail, so the honest answer is that its exact rules are not documented there. Players usually want to know whether it limits something they carry between characters, servers or versions. Rather than guess, verify it in game: check the character or transfer interface directly, and if the wording is unclear, ask in the group's community space where developers or moderators can answer.

## Races and Forms: Stick to What Is Named

*Dragon Ball Z* is the source material the developers name, so races, transformations and terminology from that series are fair to reference. What is not fair — and what you should ignore — is any specific multiplier, stat value, unlock level or damage formula presented as fact. The official page publishes none of that.

Community guides often fill the gap with numbers, and some of those numbers may be tested and reasonable. But they are observations, not specifications. They can change with a single update, and the official page gives you no changelog to check them against. When a guide and the game disagree, the game wins.

A practical habit: before committing hours to a build because a guide recommends it, confirm the prerequisite exists in your current version. Open the relevant menu, read the in-game requirements, and check whether the content is actually available in the Namek expansion rather than content you remember from the original game.

## Playing With 50-Player Servers

The advertised server size is up to 50 players. That is a meaningful design detail even without any other documentation. Large servers in an RPG usually mean shared open zones rather than instanced solo content, so expect competition for spawns, bosses or objectives during peak hours.

If the population feels crowded, the simplest variables you control are timing and server hopping through the standard Roblox interface. The official page does not document a private server product or a server browser beyond Roblox's own. Do not assume one exists.

## What Is Simply Not Documented

This is the part most articles skip. Based on the official page, the following are **not** documented:

- A promo-code system. The page lists no codes, and no developer channel is cited as publishing them. Any "active codes" list you see is unverified by the official source.
- Item prices or a premium currency.
- Skill trees, stat allocations or respec rules.
- Drop rates for any item.
- Patch notes, roadmaps or release dates for future expansions.

If you want any of that, verify it in game or through the developer group's own channels. Do not rely on an aggregator that republishes another site's guesses.

## How to Verify Anything You Read

Three checks cover almost every claim:

1. Open the [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) and read the description. It is short enough to read fully.
2. Check the last-updated date. If a guide predates a recent update, its details may already be stale.
3. Test the claim in game before you build around it. Menus and requirement text are the closest thing to ground truth you have.

None of this guarantees a fast grind or a strong character. It guarantees you are not wasting time on information the developers never published.
