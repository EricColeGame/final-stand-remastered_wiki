Final Stand Remastered is an anime-inspired RPG on Roblox, and if you have heard the name before, it is probably because of the game it is built on. The official page is clear that this is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the long-running Roblox RPG, run by the group Final Stand Remastered. The place was created on November 6, 2021, was last updated on October 12, 2026, supports up to 50 players per server, and sits at roughly 19.7 million visits. Those numbers alone tell you this is not a dead project: a four-year-old experience still receiving updates is one worth understanding before you invest time in it.

That said, the gap between what a game officially documents and what players believe about it is usually where confusion starts. This guide separates the two.

## What the official page actually confirms

The official Roblox listing for [Final Stand Remastered](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) is short and specific. It states that the game is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, that the current expansion is Namek, that the level cap is 305, and that the transfer cap is 125. It also credits the development team by role, listing a lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator.

That is the full extent of what is officially advertised. It is worth pausing on that, because a lot of what circulates about games like this online is not on the official page at all. The listing does not document a promo-code system, item prices, skill trees, drop rates, stat values, multipliers, quest steps, or unlock levels. If you see those things presented as fact elsewhere, they are coming from community sources, not the developer.

The 50-player server cap and the 19.7 million visit count are useful context. A 50-player limit is generous for a Roblox RPG, which means world bosses, open-world encounters, and shared leveling areas can actually feel populated. The visit count suggests a stable, if not explosive, audience.

## The Namek expansion and what an expansion structure means

The word "expansion" in the official description is doing real work. It signals that the game is organized into content phases rather than being a single flat world, and that Namek is the current one. In Dragon Ball Z terms, Namek is the arc most players associate with the Frieza conflict, and it is a natural second major setting after Earth.

What an expansion structure implies practically:

- Progression is likely gated by content phase, so a new player will not be dropped straight into endgame territory.
- The level cap of 305 is the ceiling for the current state of the game, not a permanent number. Caps rise when expansions or updates change the content ceiling.
- The transfer cap of 125 is a separate figure from the level cap, and the official page does not explain how the two interact. That distinction matters and is worth verifying in-game rather than assuming.

If you are trying to plan a character around level 305, understand that the official page tells you the destination, not the route.

## Races, forms, and reading third-party guides correctly

Dragon Ball Z is the source material the developers name, so races and transformations from that universe are fair to discuss. Third-party guides commonly describe a race selection step at character creation and transformation paths tied to progression. Treat all of that as secondary observation, not developer documentation. A community guide can tell you what players generally do; it cannot tell you a verified multiplier or an exact unlock level unless the developer published it.

A reasonable way to use third-party material:

1. Use it to understand the shape of the game: what races broadly exist, what roles transformations play, how PvE and PvP are structured.
2. Verify every specific number in-game before building around it. Numbers in community guides go stale after updates, and this experience was last updated on October 12, 2026, so any guide written before that date may already be wrong.
3. When a guide and the official page disagree, trust the official page for identity-level facts like the expansion name and the caps.
