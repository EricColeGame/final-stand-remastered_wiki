## What Final Stand Remastered Officially Is

Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox experience created by the group Final Stand Remastered. The official description states plainly that it is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the long-running Roblox RPG. That sentence matters because it explains the genre: this is an anime-inspired RPG built around races, leveling, and an expansion structure, not a fighting game or a simulator.

The place was created on November 6, 2021, and the official record shows it was last updated on October 12, 2026. It supports up to 50 players per server and has accumulated roughly 19.7 million visits. Those numbers describe scale and activity, not quality, but they do tell you the experience is actively maintained rather than abandoned — the update date is only two days before this article's date.

The official page also credits the development team by role, including the lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and animator. That is worth noting because it signals a structured team behind the remaster rather than a single hobbyist uploading a copy.

## The Advertised Features: Namek, Level Cap 305, Transfer Cap 125

This is the part most third-party pages get wrong, so it is worth stating carefully. According to the official description, three concrete facts are advertised:

- The current expansion is Namek.
- The level cap is 305.
- The transfer cap is 125.

That is the entire documented feature set. The official page does not list a full roadmap, a list of future expansions, or how long the current one will run.

A useful way to think about the two caps is that they answer different questions.

| Advertised figure | What it constrains |
|---|---|
| Level cap 305 | The highest character level the current expansion supports |
| Transfer cap 125 | The limit on what carries over when transferring a character |
| Current expansion: Namek | The content arc the game is presently built around |

If you are planning a long-term character, the transfer cap is arguably the more important number, because it governs what survives a transfer rather than what you can reach right now. But the official page states only the number — it does not spell out transfer rules, cooldowns, costs, or exactly which assets count toward the cap. Do not assume a third-party guide knows either.

## Community Observations vs. Official Documentation

Here is where you need to be careful. A large amount of Final Stand Remastered content circulating online — race tier lists, form multipliers, quest walkthroughs, grinding routes, drop tables — comes from community guides and wiki-style sites. These are secondary observations, not developer documentation. They can be genuinely useful, and many are written by experienced players, but they are not authoritative. They go stale after updates, and the game was updated on October 12, 2026, which is recent enough to invalidate older guides.

Treat community content as a starting hypothesis. Then verify in game. If a guide tells you a race scales a certain way, you can confirm the actual behavior yourself by leveling and comparing. If it claims a specific quest gives a specific reward, run the quest and look at what you receive.

## Things That Are Simply Not Documented

This is the section most articles skip, and it is the most honest one. The official Roblox page for Final Stand Remastered does **not** document:

- Any promo code system. If you see a "working codes" list for this game, the official page does not back it up. The game may or may not have codes; the official page documents none, and this article will not pretend otherwise.
- Item prices, shop inventories, or currency systems.
- Skill trees, stat allocations, or respec rules.
- Drop rates for any item.
- Hotkeys, controls, or UI layouts.
- Patch notes or a changelog.
- Release dates for future expansions.

That list is not a criticism of the developers — Roblox descriptions are short by design. It is a warning about the ecosystem. When a guide states a precise drop rate or a code that "definitely works," ask where that number came from. If the answer is another guide, you have not found a source.

The practical alternative: launch the game, open the relevant menu, and read. The in-game shop shows its own prices. The in-game stat screen shows its own numbers. That is faster and more reliable than any article, including this one.

## A Note on Picking a Race or Build

Final Stand Remastered is built on Dragon Ball Z, and the source material's races and transformations are the natural vocabulary of the game. But the official page does not publish race stats, form multipliers, or unlock levels. Any article that gives you exact numbers for those is either drawing on datamined files, personal testing, or guesswork — and it usually will not tell you which.

If you want a build recommendation, the honest version is: pick a race whose theme you enjoy, because you will spend many hours with it, then adjust based on what you observe in game. Community consensus shifts with every balance update. A build that was dominant before the October 2026 update may not be now.
