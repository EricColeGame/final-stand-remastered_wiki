Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox RPG that wears its lineage openly. According to its official page, it is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, a well-known Roblox RPG built around the Dragon Ball Z universe. That single sentence tells you a lot: this is a remaster project, not a spin-off with a borrowed name, and the developers say so themselves.

If you are deciding whether to invest time in it, the useful question is not "is this like the original?" but "what does the official record actually promise, and what is left for me to verify in-game?" This guide separates those two things carefully.

## What the Official Page Confirms

The official Roblox listing for [Final Stand Remastered](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) is short and specific. It states that the experience is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, identifies the current expansion as Namek, and lists a level cap of 305 with a transfer cap of 125.

The listing also credits the development team by role: a lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists and an animator. That is worth noting because credit lists are one of the clearest signals that a project is actively staffed rather than abandoned.

On the technical side, the same record shows the place was created on November 6, 2021, last updated on October 12, 2026, supports up to 50 players per server, and had accumulated roughly 19.7 million visits as of October 14, 2026. The creator is the Roblox group Final Stand Remastered.

## What the Expansion Structure Tells You

The word "expansion" is doing real work here. The official page names Namek as the current expansion, which implies a structure where content arrives in labeled chunks rather than as one fixed world. It does not tell you how many expansions exist, what order they run in, or what level range each one covers.

This matters if you are planning a character. A level cap of 305 combined with an expansion called Namek suggests — but does not confirm — that Namek content is where a lot of late progression happens. The official page does not describe quest flow, so treat any community claim about "Namek unlocks at level X" as third-party guidance until you can check it yourself in-game.

The transfer cap of 125 is the more unusual number. Caps of this kind typically govern how much progress or currency can move between characters, servers, or formats — but the official description does not define what "transfer" means in this game. If that distinction matters to you, the fastest honest answer is to ask in-game or check the developer group, not to trust a guide that states a definition as fact.

## Races, Forms, and What Is Not Documented

Dragon Ball Z is the source material the developers name, so races and transformation forms are a reasonable expectation for an anime-inspired RPG. The credited community documentation names specific races and forms drawn from that universe.

## How to Build a Character Without Guessing

The safest approach is to treat the advertised numbers as your only hard constraints and let the game fill in the rest. You know the ceiling is 305 and that a transfer cap of 125 exists. You know Namek is the current expansion. You do not know which race performs best, which form to chase, or where experience is densest.

To resolve those unknowns yourself:

- Read the in-game UI for stat descriptions and unlock prompts, which reflect the current build rather than a snapshot from months ago.
- Check the official game page for description updates, since expansion names and caps change as the remaster progresses.
- Look at the credited team roles if you want to gauge how much content and art is actively being produced.
- Test mechanics on a character you do not mind rebuilding, especially where a transfer cap might apply.

Community build advice can still be useful, but treat it as a starting hypothesis rather than a recipe. No guide — this one included — can guarantee results, because the developers have not published the underlying numbers.
