Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox experience that does something relatively unusual on the platform: it rebuilds an older, well-known Roblox RPG from the ground up rather than simply patching it. According to its official Roblox page, it is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, and it is developed by the group Final Stand Remastered. That page — the authoritative source for what the game actually advertises — is where any serious player should start, at https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered.

The problem most players run into is not a lack of information; it is a surplus of unverified information. Third-party wikis, YouTube videos, and Discord rumor threads mix developer-confirmed details with community guesswork, and the two are rarely labeled. This guide separates them deliberately.

## Understanding the Expansion Structure

The word "expansion" is doing real work in that description. Final Stand Remastered is built as a progression through content tiers, and the official page identifies Namek as the current one. That framing tells you two things: the game has a defined end point for its present content, and the developers intend to extend it.

An anime-inspired RPG with races, leveling, and an expansion structure means your character's race choice and your level progress are the two long-term decisions that shape everything else. Community guides generally treat race selection as the most consequential early decision because it is the hardest to reverse. That is a community framing, not an official statement — the official page does not publish race-by-race comparisons, and any numbers you see for them come from player testing rather than developer documentation.

The level cap of 305 is your practical target ceiling. The transfer cap of 125 is a separate constraint, and it is the number players most often misread. Do not assume it maps cleanly onto a level threshold without checking in game, because the official page does not explain its mechanics.

## Leveling Toward the Level Cap

Because no official leveling table exists, the honest advice is procedural rather than numeric:

- Check the official page after each major update. The cap has moved before and can move again; a guide pinned to an old cap wastes your time.
- Play the expansion content that is actually current. Namek is advertised as the active expansion, so content outside it is likely legacy or transitional.
- Use community sources for routing, not for numbers. A third-party guide can tell you a sensible order to attempt content; it cannot authoritatively tell you a drop rate.
- Verify anything suspicious in game before you build a plan around it.

That last point deserves emphasis. Roblox experiences update server-side, and community documentation lags behind updates by days or weeks. When a guide and the live game disagree, the live game is correct.

## Community Observations Versus Documented Facts

A useful habit is sorting every claim you encounter into one of three buckets:

**Documented.** The Namek expansion, the 305 level cap, the 125 transfer cap, the 50-player servers, and the development credits all come straight from the official Roblox record. You can cite these confidently.

**Community-observed.** Build recommendations, race tier lists, grinding routes, and boss strategies circulate widely. These are secondary observations from players, not developer documentation. They are often genuinely useful and often outdated at the same time.

**Undocumented.** Promo codes, item prices, skill tree layouts, drop rates, exact stat values, and patch notes are not on the official page. This does not automatically mean they do not exist — only that they are not developer-confirmed in the record we can verify. If you want to know, test in game or watch official developer channels.

If you played the original Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the remaster is not simply the same game with better graphics. The official description's decision to name the source game explicitly signals continuity of setting and identity while the underlying experience is rebuilt in a separate place. Dragon Ball Z is the source material the developers themselves name. Terms, races, and forms drawn from it are fair to discuss. Invented stat values and unlock levels are not, and the official page supplies none.
