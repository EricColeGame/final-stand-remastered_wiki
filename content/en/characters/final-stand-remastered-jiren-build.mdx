Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox RPG that carries a recognizable name in the anime-inspired corner of the platform. Its official page is unusually direct about what it is: a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand. If you are deciding whether to start a character, return after a long break, or gauge how much time the grind expects from you, the most useful thing you can do is separate the advertised facts from community folklore. That distinction is the whole point of this article.

## What the official page actually advertises

The [Final Stand Remastered experience](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) is published by the Roblox group Final Stand Remastered. The place was created on November 6, 2021, and the official record showed it last updated on October 12, 2026, with roughly 19.7 million visits and servers supporting up to 50 players. Those numbers describe popularity and scale, not difficulty, so treat them as context rather than a measure of how long any goal will take.

The official description states several concrete things worth memorizing:

- It is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand.
- The current expansion is Namek.
- The level cap is 305.
- The transfer cap is 125.

That is a short list, and that brevity matters. The same description credits the development team, including the lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists and animator. What it does not include is just as important: there is no documented promo-code system, no item prices, no skill trees and no drop rates on the official page.

You can confirm the current numbers yourself by opening the game page and reading the description, then checking your own character screen in game.

## Races, forms and progression: what is documented and what is not

Dragon Ball Z is the source material the developers name, and the credited wiki and official page together cover races and forms as vocabulary. What they do not provide is a stat sheet. There are no published multipliers for transformations, no quest-by-quest unlock levels, and no verified drop tables. Any guide that hands you a precise multiplier or an exact unlock level is offering a community estimate, not developer documentation.

That does not make third-party guides useless. It makes them secondary. A community guide is a record of what players observed on a given build, and builds change, especially when an expansion is live. A reasonable habit is to treat any specific number from a third-party guide as a hypothesis you test in your own session rather than a fact you build a plan around. If a guide conflicts with the official page on something the page does state, such as the level cap or the expansion name, trust the page.

## How to approach the Namek expansion without guessing

Because Namek is the advertised current expansion, that is where the official framing points your attention. Beyond that, the page is silent on quest steps, enemy levels and rewards, so the reliable method is observation:

1. Read the official description first so you know the current cap and expansion.
2. Create a character and check the in-game menus for level, race and transfer options rather than relying on a video description.
3. Ask other players in a server which activities they are currently running, and compare answers across a few sessions.
4. Re-check the place page after a major update, since the numbers you memorized can change.

If a claim cannot be reproduced in the game and is not on the official page, file it as unverified. That includes any mention of codes. Since the official page documents no promo-code system, you should assume none is documented and verify in game before spending time hunting for one.

## Reading the Roblox page like a source

Roblox place pages are living documents. The visit count, the last-updated stamp and even the description can shift between updates. When you open the experience page, look for the group name to confirm you are on the right place rather than a similarly named copy, then scan the description for the expansion name and the two cap numbers. That thirty-second check protects you from advice written for a different game or an older build.
