Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox experience that wears its lineage openly. Its official page describes it as a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the long-running anime-inspired RPG, and the game has been live under the Roblox group Final Stand Remastered since the place was created on November 6, 2021. By the last recorded update on October 12, 2026, it had accumulated roughly 19.7 million visits and supports up to 50 players per server. If you are deciding whether to start, return, or bring a friend in, the useful question is not "is this like the original?" but "what does the current version actually advertise, and what do I have to check myself?"

This guide separates three things that often get blurred together in Roblox RPG coverage: what the developer page states, what third-party guides report, and what is simply undocumented. It does not promise that any build or route guarantees success, because the official record does not publish that kind of detail.

## What the official page actually confirms

There is a surprisingly small but firm set of facts the official description commits to. Treat these as the baseline:

- It is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand.
- The current expansion is Namek.
- The level cap is 305.
- The transfer cap is 125.
- The development team is credited by role: lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists and an animator.
- Up to 50 players share a server; the place was created in 2021 and last updated in October 2026.

That is the advertised feature set. Notice what is absent. The official page does not document a promo-code system, item prices, skill trees, drop rates, hotkeys, or patch notes. If a video or listicle claims a specific code or a precise multiplier, that claim is not coming from the developer record, and you should treat it as unverified until you confirm it in game.

A useful habit: [open the official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) and read the description yourself rather than trusting a summary, including this one. Developers update the expansion name, caps and credits there first.

## Why the level cap and transfer cap matter together

Two numbers do most of the work in shaping how you play: a level cap of 305 and a transfer cap of 125.

The level cap tells you where progression stops. It does not tell you how fast you get there, what each level grants, or which activities are most efficient, because the official page does not publish those rates. Any "best XP route" advice is community observation and should be treated as a starting hypothesis, not a rule.

The transfer cap is the more interesting constraint. In remaster-style RPGs built around an expansion structure, a transfer cap usually gates how much progress can carry across a boundary. The official page states the number but does not spell out the mechanics. Practical reasoning follows from that: before you invest heavily in one character or race, confirm in game what actually transfers and what does not. Do that check before a long grind, not after.

## Races, forms and how to verify them

Because the developers name Dragon Ball Z as the source material, terms like races and transformations are part of the game's vocabulary. The official page does not list the races, their stat differences, or their unlock conditions, and this article will not invent them. What you can do is verify them directly:

1. Create a character and read the race selection screen in full before committing.
2. Check whether race choice is permanent or reversible, and whether it interacts with the transfer cap.
3. Confirm which forms exist by playing to the relevant milestone or by asking in game, not by trusting an uncredited chart.
4. Re-check after an update, since a remaster's balance can shift between versions.

Third-party guides do describe races, forms and leveling paths, and they are sometimes accurate. But they are secondary observations written by players, not developer documentation. Attribute them that way in your own head: helpful, fallible, and worth a five-minute in-game test before you build a plan around them.

## Expansion structure and the Namek era

The expansion framing is the most important structural fact here. A game with a named current expansion is a game that expects content to arrive in chapters rather than all at once. Namek being "current" implies it is where the live content focus sits, and it implies older content still exists underneath it.

Practically, that shapes three decisions:

- Start now rather than waiting. The cap is published at 305, so there is a defined ceiling to work toward, and a 50-player server size means the world stays populated without being unplayable.
- Expect the meta to move. When an expansion advances, the value of older grinds can change. Community advice written for a previous expansion may no longer be optimal.
- Read the description after major updates. The last recorded update was October 12, 2026, two days before this article's date, which is a reminder that the official page is a living document.

## Codes, prices and other undocumented claims

This is where most Final Stand Remastered search results go wrong. The official page documents no promo-code system. That does not prove codes will never exist; it means there is no developer-documented code list to redeem, so any "active codes" article is either guessing, recycling expired entries, or inventing them. If you want to know whether a code feature exists, look for an in-game redeem box or an announcement from the developer group, then test one code and report the result. Do not spend time on a list you cannot trace to a developer source.

The same discipline applies to item prices, drop rates, skill tree layouts and stat multipliers. None are on the official record. If a guide gives you a number, ask where it came from. If the answer is "a video" or "a Discord screenshot," that is community data, and community data goes stale fast in an actively updated remaster.
