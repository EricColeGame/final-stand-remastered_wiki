## What the Official Page Confirms

The official Roblox record for [Final Stand Remastered](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) is unusually clear about the game's identity. It states that this is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, an older Roblox RPG with a long-running player base. The creator is listed as the group Final Stand Remastered rather than an individual account, which matters if you are checking whether a page or Discord is genuinely connected to the project.

The place was created on November 6, 2021, and was last updated on October 12, 2026, two days before this article's date. Servers support up to 50 players. The page shows roughly 19.7 million visits at the time of retrieval. Those numbers describe scale and activity, not quality — a high visit count on a remaster usually reflects the audience the original game built, not a fresh verdict on the new version.

Most usefully, the description names the current expansion: Namek. It also states a level cap of 305 and a transfer cap of 125. Those three figures are the backbone of any planning you do, because they tell you where progression currently stops and how much carries over between expansions.

## Level Cap 305 and Transfer Cap 125

The distinction between the level cap and the transfer cap is the single most practical thing to understand about this game. The level cap is the ceiling you can currently reach. The transfer cap is the ceiling on what carries forward when content moves to a new expansion. A common structure in expansion-based RPGs is that excess levels are trimmed or converted at transition, so the transfer cap effectively determines how much of your grind survives into the next chapter.

That combination is the genre template: pick a race with different baseline tendencies, level through PvE content, and unlock transformations as you progress through expansions.

The official page does not publish a race list with stat differences, a transformation unlock table, or any numeric multipliers. Community guides do discuss races and forms because the source material makes those categories obvious, but their specific numbers are third-party observations, not developer documentation. When a community guide and the official page disagree on a cap or a name, trust the official page.

## Practical Approach for New Players

Start by reading the in-game tutorial or the starting area prompts rather than a guide written for a different build of the game. A remaster changes systems between versions, and any guide with a date older than the last update on October 12, 2026 may describe something that no longer exists.

Level with the transfer cap in mind. If only 125 levels carry forward, the value of grinding far beyond the point where you have unlocked what you want is genuinely unclear, and the official page does not explain the conversion. Ask in-game or in the group's community space, and treat the answer as player knowledge rather than an official rule.

Verify anything you intend to spend time or Robux on inside the game's own store interface. The official page lists no item prices, so any figure quoted elsewhere is unconfirmed. And keep the official listing bookmarked: it is where a cap change or a new expansion would appear first.
