Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox anime RPG that reworks the long-running Dragon Ball Z Final Stand formula. If you have seen the name in search results and wondered whether it is a separate game, a sequel, or just a re-upload, the answer is on the official listing. According to the official Roblox page, [Final Stand Remastered](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, run by the Roblox group Final Stand Remastered. The place was created on November 6, 2021, and it supports up to 50 players per server.

What makes this page useful is not hype. It is that the official description is unusually specific about where the game currently stands: the active expansion is Namek, the level cap is 305, and the transfer cap is 125. Those three numbers do more to set expectations than any trailer. This article separates what the developers advertise from what the community reports, and it flags the things that are simply not documented anywhere official so you know when to stop trusting a random guide.

## What the official page actually confirms

The official Roblox record is the authority for the game's identity. It states plainly that this is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, not a new IP and not an unrelated project that borrowed the name. It lists the current expansion as Namek, which tells you the game is being developed in expansion arcs rather than as one finished, permanent world.

It also publishes the progression ceilings: level cap 305 and transfer cap 125. Those are the numbers to plan around. If a guide tells you to push past 305 or to transfer more than 125, that guide is either outdated or describing something the official page does not support.

The page credits the development team, including the lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator. That matters for one practical reason: when you want to know whether a feature is real, the credited team's own channels and the official page carry more weight than an aggregator that scraped a screenshot.

## Namek, expansion structure, and what it implies

An "expansion" label is a design signal. It means content is added in batches, and the current batch is Namek. For a returning player, the useful question is not "what is the best build" but "does my existing progress still make sense under the current cap."

Because the official description names Namek as the current expansion while also setting a level cap of 305, it is reasonable to treat the game as living content rather than a finished product. That is a design statement the developers made themselves. It is not a claim that older content was removed, and it is not a promise about what comes next.

One thing the official page does not do is publish a roadmap with dates. If you see a "confirmed" future expansion with a release window, treat that as unverified unless it appears on the official page or the developers' own channels.

## How to verify anything you read in a guide

Third-party guides are useful for orientation and frustrating for precision. The reliable method is short and boring:

1. Open the [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) and check the description first. Caps and the current expansion are stated there.
2. Join a server and read the in-game UI. Level requirements, race options, and any transfer interface are visible to you without a guide.
3. If a guide and the game disagree, trust the game. Guides age; live builds do not.
4. If something appears in no official place and in no in-game text, assume it is undocumented rather than secret.

That fourth point is the important one. Code lists are a common example. Many Roblox games run promo codes; the official Final Stand Remastered description does not document a code system at all. That does not prove codes will never exist, but it does mean any code list you find is unverified. Test it in game, and do not build a plan around it.

## Practical advice for a new or returning player

Play the current expansion rather than chasing a future one. The official page tells you Namek is active and the level cap is 305, so your progression target is already defined. There is no need to speculate about what comes after.

Respect the ceilings. A cap of 305 and a transfer cap of 125 are the developers telling you where the current design ends. Guides that promise a way around either number are describing something the sources do not support.

Pick a race based on what you enjoy, not on a tier list you cannot verify. The official page confirms that races exist as part of the RPG structure, but it does not publish stat values or multipliers. Community tier lists are opinions shaped by whatever build the author plays. They can be a starting point, never a verdict.

Finally, use the server size. With up to 50 players per server, this is a social game by design. Asking in chat about a mechanic is often faster and more accurate than reading a guide written months ago. No method guarantees success, and no article, including this one, can promise a specific outcome. What you can do is ground your expectations in the official record and check the live game before you commit time or items.
