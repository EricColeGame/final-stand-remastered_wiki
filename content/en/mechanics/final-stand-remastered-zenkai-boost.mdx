Final Stand: Remastered is a Roblox RPG that carries a familiar name. According to its official Roblox page, it is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, developed by the group Final Stand Remastered. The place was created on November 6, 2021, and was last updated on October 12, 2026, which tells you something useful right away: this is a game that is still being worked on, not a finished artifact frozen in time. The same record shows servers of up to 50 players and roughly 19.7 million visits.

That combination of an old, well-known RPG and an active remaster is exactly why players search for reliable information and end up frustrated. This article sticks to what the official page states, labels community advice as community advice, and says plainly where the record is silent.

## What the official page actually documents

The official description is short, and it is worth treating as the ceiling of what anyone can state as fact about the game's advertised structure. It states:

- The experience is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand.
- The current expansion is Namek.
- The level cap is 305.
- The transfer cap is 125.
- The development team is credited, including a lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator.

That is the whole of it. There is no promo-code list, no item pricing, no skill tree breakdown, no drop rate table, and no quest walkthrough on the official page. If you see a site claiming to have pulled those numbers "from the developers," be skeptical unless the claim links to a developer channel rather than a fan page.

## Why the level cap and transfer cap matter

The two numbers most players will encounter early are the level cap of 305 and the transfer cap of 125. The level cap does exactly what it sounds like: it is the advertised maximum character level in the current build.

The transfer cap is more interesting because it is the kind of number that shapes long-term decisions. A cap of 125 implies there is some transfer mechanic that stops accepting progress past a certain point. The official page does not explain what transfers, what the cost is, or what happens when you reach that cap. Anything beyond the number itself is undocumented.

If you want to know how the transfer cap behaves in practice, the reliable method is to check in-game. Open the relevant menu, read the tooltip or requirement text, and note whether the cap applies to currency, items, or progression. Do not trust a number you read on an aggregator page over the number the game itself displays.

## Races and forms: what you can reasonably assume

The developers name Dragon Ball Z as their source material, and the credited community wiki documents the races and transformations the game draws from. That means terms like Saiyan, Namekian, and the various transformation tiers are part of the game's vocabulary in a general sense.

What is not documented is how those races perform. There are no official multipliers, no confirmed stat growth curves, and no developer-published tier list. Any guide that tells you one race is objectively strongest is offering a community opinion, not a developer fact. That does not make it worthless; experienced players often have good reasons for their preferences. It does mean you should treat those claims as observations that can change between updates.

The last-updated date of October 12, 2026 is the reason for caution here. A game that is actively patched can shift balance between the time a guide is written and the time you read it. Verify anything numeric in-game before you commit hours to a build.

## Getting started without bad information

The official Roblox page is the single best starting point because it is current and it is authored by the developers. You can find it at https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered. Before you play, read the description there, then check how recently the page was updated so you know whether you are reading a stale copy mirrored elsewhere.

A practical approach for new players:

1. Join a server, keeping the 50-player limit in mind for crowded events.
2. Confirm the current expansion by reading in-game notices rather than assuming Namek is still the newest content.
3. Check your own level against the 305 cap before planning a long grind.
4. If you intend to use the transfer system, find the in-game explanation of the 125 cap first.

None of this guarantees a fast or easy progression. It simply reduces the chance that you build around numbers someone invented.

## What is simply not documented

This is the section most guides skip. The following are not documented on the official page, and no amount of confident phrasing changes that:

Promo codes: The official page documents none. Some Roblox games use codes heavily; this one's official record does not advertise a code system. If codes exist, the developer channels would be the place they appear, not a fan wiki.

Prices and shop stock: Not published officially. In-game shops display their own prices.

Drop rates: Not published. Community estimates exist but are estimates.

Skill trees and stat scaling: Not published in the official description.

Patch notes and release dates: Not on the Roblox page. Look for developer announcements.

If you need any of these, verify them inside the game or through official developer communications. That is not a dodge; it is the only honest answer when the source material does not contain the information.
