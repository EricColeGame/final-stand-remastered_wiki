If you played the original Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the name Final Stand Remastered probably already tells you what you are looking at. The important thing to understand before you invest hours is that the official Roblox listing is unusually specific about some things and silent about others — and that gap is where most player confusion starts.

This article sticks to what the developer-controlled page actually states, labels community knowledge as community knowledge, and is honest about what nobody outside the team can verify yet.

## What the Official Page Confirms

According to the official Roblox record for [Final Stand Remastered](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered) (place 7922773201), the experience is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand. The creator is the group Final Stand Remastered. The place was created on November 6, 2021, and was last updated on October 12, 2026. Servers hold up to 50 players, and the experience has roughly 19.7 million visits as of October 14, 2026.

The description also credits the development team, including the lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and animator. That is a normal but meaningful detail: it means the project is maintained by a credited team rather than a single anonymous uploader, which matters for a remaster that has to keep pace with an active Roblox codebase.

## The Three Numbers That Matter Most

The official description lists a current expansion, a level cap, and a transfer cap. These are the only progression numbers the developer endorses, so treat them as the floor of what you know rather than the ceiling.

| Official detail | Stated value |
|---|---|
| Current expansion | Namek |
| Level cap | 305 |
| Transfer cap | 125 |

Two of these deserve unpacking.

The **level cap of 305** is the highest character level the game currently allows. Any guide claiming a different cap — higher or lower — is either outdated or guessing. If you see a number other than 305 on a fan site, check the official page before believing it.

The **transfer cap of 125** is the number that trips people up. In remastered anime RPGs, a "transfer" usually refers to carrying something — levels, progress, or a character — into a new expansion or server context, and a cap limits how much can move. That is exactly how the term is used across the genre. But the official page gives the number and not the full rule set, so the precise mechanics of what transfers, and under what conditions, are not documented publicly. The safest way to understand it is in-game, on your own character, where the transfer interface will show you its own limits.

## The Namek Expansion and What It Means for Play

The official page states that the current expansion is Namek. In the source material the developers name — Dragon Ball Z — Namek is the arc with the planet, the dragon balls, and the big power spikes, so an expansion carrying that name signals a content tier rather than a small update.

What the page does **not** tell you: how many quests the expansion contains, what zones it opens, what level you need before you can meaningfully engage with it, or how long it takes to complete. Those are all things a player can only confirm by playing. If a third-party guide gives you exact Namek quest steps or unlock levels, treat it as a community observation, not developer documentation — it may be accurate, but it is not authoritative, and it can break the moment the team patches something.

## Races, Forms, and RPG Structure

Final Stand Remastered is described as an anime-inspired RPG with races, leveling, and an expansion structure. The source material the developers name, Dragon Ball Z, is where the races and forms come from, and the credited community wiki documents those terms.

What is not documented on the official page: stat values, multipliers, skill trees, drop rates, or item prices. None of them appear in the listing. That is not the same as saying they do not exist — it means the developer has not published them, so any specific number you read elsewhere came from testing by players, not from the team. Numbers from testing can be right for a patch and wrong a week later.

A practical approach: learn the *shape* of the systems (races differ, levels gate content, transfers have a limit), then verify the *numbers* yourself in-game. Your own character sheet and the in-game UI are always current.

## How to Verify Anything You Read About This Game

Because so much of the useful information lives outside the official page, it helps to have a verification routine:

1. Open the official Roblox page and read the description first. It is short, and it is the only place the developer speaks directly.
2. Check the last-updated date on that page. If a guide is older than the most recent update, its numbers may be stale.
3. Confirm caps and limits in your own game menus rather than on a wiki.
4. Treat YouTube and fan wikis as secondary sources. Useful, but secondary.
5. Re-check after major updates. A remaster of a live Roblox game changes.

## What Is Simply Not Documented

To be direct about the gaps, the official page does not document a promo-code system, item prices, skill trees, or drop rates. It documents no codes at all. If you see a "working codes" list for Final Stand Remastered, that list is not backed by anything the developer has published — either the codes come from a developer channel outside the Roblox page, or the list is unreliable. Verify in-game before spending time entering anything.

Likewise, no release dates, patch notes, leaks, hotkeys, or character stats are published on the official listing. Saying "this is undocumented" is not a criticism of the game; it is the honest state of the public information, and it is the single most useful thing to internalize so you do not chase numbers that were never official.
