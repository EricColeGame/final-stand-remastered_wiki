Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox experience that wears its inspiration openly. According to its [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered), it is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, the long-running Roblox RPG built around the Dragon Ball Z anime. That single sentence tells you most of what you need to know before you ever load in: expect races, leveling, transformations borrowed from the source material, and a progression curve that rewards patience over a single afternoon.

What makes this version worth a separate look is that it is not the original experience with a fresh coat of paint. It is a distinct place, published by the group Final Stand Remastered, and it has its own development team, its own expansion schedule, and its own advertised caps. If you are deciding whether to invest time in it—or you are returning after months away and wondering what changed—the useful move is to separate what the developers actually state from what the community assumes.

## What the Official Page Actually Confirms

Roblox game pages are the closest thing to developer documentation most experiences have, so it is worth being precise about what this one says. The official description states that Final Stand Remastered is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, that the current expansion is Namek, that the level cap is 305, and that the transfer cap is 125. It also credits the development team, including a lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator.

That is the full set of advertised features. Notice what is not there: no promo codes, no item prices, no skill trees, no drop rates, no stat multipliers, no quest walkthroughs. If you see a guide promising a specific code or an exact zeni price, that claim is not coming from the official page. It may still be accurate—developers often communicate through Discord or in-game menus rather than the description—but it is not documented where you can verify it at a glance.

The page metadata is also informative in a quieter way. The place was created in November 2021 and last updated in October 2026, which tells you this is an actively maintained project rather than an abandoned remaster. Servers hold up to 50 players, and the experience has accumulated roughly 19.7 million visits. For an anime RPG on Roblox, that is a healthy, persistent player base—enough that you will rarely load into an empty server, but not so crowded that every farming spot is contested.

## The Namek Expansion and What an Expansion Means Here

The single most important line on the official page is that the current expansion is Namek. In this genre, an expansion is not a cosmetic update. It is usually a content tier: a new map or region, a raised level ceiling, new progression materials, and a set of enemies tuned for players who have already finished the previous arc. The fact that the page names a *current* expansion strongly implies there were earlier ones and that more may follow.

Because the level cap is listed at 305, players arriving from the original Dragon Ball Z Final Stand should expect a meaningfully longer grind than they remember. That said, the page does not break the cap down by expansion, does not say what level you need to access Namek, and does not describe what drops or enemies live there. If you want those specifics, the reliable sources are the in-game UI and the developer channels—not a wiki written by another player.

### Understanding the Transfer Cap

The transfer cap of 125 is the detail most likely to confuse newcomers, and it is worth slowing down on. A transfer cap in this style of RPG generally governs how much progression can be carried across some kind of boundary—a prestige system, a race change, or a transfer between game versions. The official page gives the number 125 and nothing else.

| Advertised detail | Official value | What is *not* documented |
|---|---|---|

If the transfer cap matters to your plans, do not guess. Open the game, find the relevant menu or NPC, and read the on-screen text before you commit a character to anything irreversible. That is a two-minute check that can save you a rebuild.

## Races and Forms: What You Can Reasonably Expect

The developers name Dragon Ball Z as their source material, which means the races and transformations you would recognize from that anime are fair game to discuss. Community guides for this genre typically cover the familiar roster—Saiyan-style races with staged transformations, Namekians, and others drawn from the show—but neither the official page nor its description enumerates them, and it certainly does not publish the multipliers behind each form.

This is where the line between documented and observed matters most. Community observations are genuinely useful for learning which race feels good at low levels, how a transformation is triggered, or which stats players prioritize. They are not authoritative, and they go stale after patches. The practical approach: treat third-party guides as a starting map, then confirm every number inside the game itself. If a guide tells you a form grants a specific boost and the in-game tooltip says otherwise, the tooltip wins.

## How to Verify Anything Before You Commit

Because the official page is deliberately sparse, your verification workflow matters more than any single tip. Here is a sequence that works for almost any question about Final Stand Remastered:

1. Read the in-game description of the race, form, or item directly. Tooltips are the closest thing to ground truth.
2. Check the official Roblox page for expansion and cap changes—these get updated when major content drops.
3. Cross-reference community guides, but note the date. Anything older than the most recent major update deserves skepticism.
4. Ask in the developer's official community space if one is linked. Player answers there are still player answers, but they tend to be fresher.
5. Test on a character you do not mind rebuilding if the change is irreversible.

There are no promo codes documented on the official page, so do not build a plan around redeeming one. If codes are ever added, the announcement will come from a developer channel, not from an aggregator site.
