Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox RPG that rebuilds the classic Dragon Ball Z Final Stand experience for a new generation of players. If you have heard the name thrown around in Roblox circles and want to know what the game actually is, what it advertises, and where the documented information ends and community guesswork begins, this guide walks through it carefully.

The starting point for any serious look at the game is its official listing. You can find it at [Final Stand Remastered on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/7922773201/Final-Stand-Remastered), which is the authoritative source for what the developers themselves confirm.

## What the Official Page Actually Documents

The official Roblox record is refreshingly specific about a few things, and it is worth separating those from everything else you might read.

According to the official description, Final Stand Remastered is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand. The creator is the Roblox group Final Stand Remastered, and the place was created on November 6, 2021, with its most recent update listed as October 12, 2026. Servers support up to 50 players, and the experience has accumulated roughly 19.7 million visits as of the October 14, 2026 record.

The description also names the current expansion: Namek. It states a level cap of 305 and a transfer cap of 125. Those four facts — the remaster status, the Namek expansion, the level cap, and the transfer cap — are the backbone of what is officially advertised. The page also credits the development team, including the lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator.

What the official page does not document is just as important. It does not describe a promo-code system, item prices, skill trees, drop rates, or specific stat multipliers. Anyone presenting those as official facts is going beyond what the developers have published.

## Races, Forms, and the Anime Source Material

The developers name Dragon Ball Z as the source material, and the credited wiki documents the races and forms that appear in the game. That gives players a reasonable vocabulary: you can expect the familiar anime-inspired archetypes and transformations that define the genre.

What you should not expect from this article, or from any responsible guide, is invented numbers. Stat values, multiplier percentages, and unlock levels vary by build and by patch, and none of them appear on the official page. If a friend tells you a specific race gets a specific bonus at a specific level, treat that as a community claim rather than developer documentation.

The practical way to confirm how a race or form performs is to test it in-game on your own character, or to check the in-game UI that displays your stats. That is your ground truth, not a forum post.

## The Namek Expansion and What It Means for Progression

The official page names Namek as the current expansion, which tells you where the game's attention is focused right now. Expansions in RPGs of this style typically gate content, raise the ceiling on what players can become, and shift where the population spends its time.

What the page does not tell you is which quests belong to Namek, which enemies drop what, or how long the grind takes. Those are things you learn by playing. The level cap of 305 gives you a sense of the scale: this is a long-haul RPG, not a quick session game. Reaching that ceiling is a substantial commitment, and the transfer cap of 125 suggests the developers have built in some way to carry progress or characters between contexts, though the exact mechanics are not spelled out on the listing.

## Community Observations vs. Developer Documentation

Third-party guides are useful, but they are secondary observations. They reflect what a particular player experienced on a particular patch, and they can go stale the moment the developers push an update. When a guide and the official page disagree, the official page wins.

That distinction matters most for anything involving numbers. A guide might claim a certain training method gives a certain rate, or that a specific form multiplies damage by a specific figure. Unless the developers have published that figure, it is an estimate at best. Use third-party material for orientation — where to go, what to try next — and use the game itself for verification.

## Playing Smart Without Invented Shortcuts

Because the official page documents no codes, you should be skeptical of any list promising free rewards. Some Roblox experiences do run code systems; this one's official listing does not describe one, so there is nothing official to redeem. If that changes, the developers would announce it, and the listing would be the place to check.

For progression, the sensible approach is to pick a race and form that fits how you like to play, level steadily toward the 305 cap, and explore the Namek content the developers have flagged as current. The transfer cap of 125 is worth understanding before you commit to a long-term plan, so read whatever the game itself tells you about it rather than trusting a summary.

Finally, remember that servers hold up to 50 players. Larger populations mean more competition for spawns and more potential for lag, so if you are grinding seriously, a quieter server can be a practical choice. None of this guarantees success — it is a long RPG, and progress depends on your own time and consistency.
