Final Stand Remastered is a Roblox RPG with a long memory. It is a remaster of Dragon Ball Z Final Stand, an experience that built a following years before this version appeared, and it carries that history into a newer codebase and a broader expansion structure. If you are deciding whether to invest time in it, the useful question is not "is it good" but "what does it actually ask of me." The official page answers more of that than most players realize.

Three of those numbers matter more than the rest. The level cap of 305 tells you the intended ceiling for progression right now. The transfer cap of 125 is the ceiling that applies when moving a character between save contexts, and it is deliberately lower than the level cap, which means a transferred character is not the same as a fully leveled one. The Namek expansion is the current content frame, which places the game's present arc in the Namek saga of the source material rather than in an earlier or later one.

The page also credits the development team by role: a lead designer, programmers, designers, modellers, artists, and an animator. That is a real production structure, not a solo project, and it is a reasonable signal about how often the experience gets updated. The last-updated date supports that read.

What the page does not state is equally important. There is no documentation of a promo-code system, no item prices, no skill trees, and no drop rates. If you see those things asserted confidently elsewhere, they are coming from community sources, not from the developers.

## Reading the level and transfer caps correctly

A level cap of 305 combined with a transfer cap of 125 creates a specific kind of progression. The gap between the two numbers is where most of the game's long-term time investment lives. If your character is above the transfer cap, you should expect that transferring is not a like-for-like move, and you should verify the current rules in-game before committing to anything irreversible.

This is the single most common place players get burned, and it is not because the rule is hidden. It is because players assume the two caps are the same thing, or assume a transfer preserves everything. The official numbers say otherwise. Check the in-game transfer screen, which will show you the operative limit at the moment you use it, rather than a guide written months earlier.

The practical implication: treat a transferred character as a starting point for a build, not a finished one. That reframes how you spend early time and reduces the frustration of discovering a ceiling you did not plan for.

## Making progress without wasting time

A few habits make the Namek-era game less punishing, and none of them require secret knowledge.

First, know where you sit relative to 125 and 305. Before you commit to a transfer, a reroll, or a long grind, check your current level against both numbers. The distance to the transfer cap is a different planning problem than the distance to the level cap.

Second, do not chase content that the update cycle can invalidate. With a live game that was updated recently, the safest investments are the ones tied to your character and your understanding of the systems, not to a specific farming route someone posted last year.

Third, use the server size. Fifty players per server is large. That means group content, social progression, and player-run knowledge are genuinely available to you. Asking in-game is often faster and more accurate than searching for a guide, especially for anything involving current drop behavior or quest routing.

Fourth, treat any claim about codes, prices, or drop rates as unverified until you see it in the game. The official page documents none of them, so anything you read is either community testing or guesswork, and you cannot tell which from the outside.
