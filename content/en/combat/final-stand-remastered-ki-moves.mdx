## How to read "level cap 305" and "transfer cap 125"

Two numbers on the official page do most of the work when you are planning a character. The level cap of 305 is the advertised ceiling for progression in the current build, which means the effective question for most players is not "how high can I go" but "how do I spend the 305 levels I have."

The transfer cap of 125 is the more interesting figure, and it is worth being honest about what the sources do and do not say. In remastered RPGs of this style, a transfer cap generally describes the ceiling on what can carry over when a character is moved between expansions or seasons, and the fact that it is lower than the level cap implies a deliberate separation between your current character and whatever you bring forward. That reading is reasonable, but it is an inference. The official page lists the number without explaining its mechanics. If you want to know exactly what transfers and what does not, the reliable methods are reading the in-game prompts when the transfer option appears and checking the official page for updates after each patch.

2. **Treat the level cap as a planning horizon, not a goal.** With a ceiling of 305, decisions made in the early game shape a long stretch of play, so experiment before committing to a build.
3. **Ask about transfers before you rely on them.** The transfer cap of 125 is the number most likely to affect your long-term plan, and its exact behavior is not documented publicly.
4. **Prefer in-game sources over screenshots and videos.** A guide written for an older build may describe systems that no longer match the current expansion.
5. **Ignore code lists.** The official page documents no promo-code system, so any "working codes" list is either guessing or repurposing something the developers never published.
